Despite being expressed by different genes, Nckl and Nck2 in humans share 68% amino acid identity and 79% similarity (Figure 1A) and possess both conserved and distinct downstream signaling partners (Chen et al., 1998; Lettau et al., 2009; Figure 1B). The C-terminal SH2 domain in Nck binds to specific phosphotyrosine residues (pTyr) at dynamic signaling hubs, such as activated growth factor receptors and sites of cell adhesion (Becker et al., 2000). Through its three tandem SH3 domains, Nck interacts with proteins containing proline-rich regions to form a local signaling scaffold to recruit proteins involved in cytoskeletal remodeling and regulation of gene expression (Pawson, 1995; McCarty, 1998; Lettau et al., 2009; Chaki and Rivera, 2013).
