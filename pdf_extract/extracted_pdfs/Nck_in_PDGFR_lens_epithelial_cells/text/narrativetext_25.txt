Nck is a widely expressed adaptor protein composed of one SH2-domain and three SH3 domains (SH3.1, SH3.2 and $H3.3)”°. Nck plays a pivotal role in actin reorganization, cell movement and cell adhesion”*. Nck has two highly related members, Nck1 (Ncka) and Nck2 (NckB), which share 68% amino acid identity’’. However, the non-redundant function of both Nck isoforms in humans has been shown in which Nck1, rather than Nck2, plays a significant role in CD3e-mediated T cell activation**”°. After PDGFR ligation, receptor signaling is transmitted to activate several intracellular signaling cascades, including the mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) and the phosphatidylinositol 3-kinase-Akt (PI3K-Akt) pathways. It has been shown that Nck protein acts as a linker to recruit and activate other signaling proteins and transmembrane receptors in multiple intracellular pathways”’. Nevertheless, the relation in cellular mechanism between PDGER and Nck in LEC has not been established. It has been revealed that Nck1 downregulation impairs several downstream signaling cascades*!”>”®, Moreover, Nck1 is essential for regulating actin polymerization”*. From these findings, Nck1 adaptor protein related with PDGER signaling might be the target to open new opportunities to prevent lens-related disease.
