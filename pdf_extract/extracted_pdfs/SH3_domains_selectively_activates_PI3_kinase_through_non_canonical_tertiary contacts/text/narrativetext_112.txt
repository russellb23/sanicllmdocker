Asa control experiment, we titrated the 'N-labelled SH3 domains onto unla- belled PR1 under the same conditions (Supplementary Fig. 6). At the highest p85ASH3:SH3 ratios (1:0.1), most chemical shifts disappeared in all var- iants, indicating the firm engagement of the SH3 domain in a large complex (Fig. 4C, D, F; Supplementary Fig. 5). The persistence of some signals even at minimal SH3 concentrations suggested that signal loss reflects complex formation rather than sensitivity limitations. In agreement, SH3 chemical shifts did not disappear in the control titrations with PR1 (Supplementary Fig. 6). In the p85ASH3 titrations, only residues V84, D100 and L101 for Fyn WT and D78 and T85 for Fyn R96W remained visible, suggesting these residues are highly dynamic and not restricted by the complex. As the p85ASH3:SH3 ratios decreased, more residues gradually reappeared. At low SH3 concentrations, we observed peaks for specific (flexible) residues that were not broadened and slightly shifted from their position in free SH3, indicating a fast exchange regime. At p85ASH3:SH3 ratios of 1:6 (Fyn WT and R96I) or 1:8 (Fyn R96W) most peaks reappeared (Supplementary Fig. 5). However, some residues remained invisible and only reappeared later in the titrations, indicating their chemical environment is highly per- turbed in the complex. These residues are either located near the binding site or undergo significant structural changes when bound to p85ASH3 (Fig. 4C, D, F). For all Fyn variants, position 96 was among the strongly bound residues, confirming its importance for the association with p85ASH3. This NMR experiment also identified Fyn L112 as strongly bound, even though it is remote from the PR binding surface. Interestingly, this position also experienced chemical shift changes upon binding of the short PR1 peptide which cannot reach L112 (Fig. 4A, B, E), suggesting an allosteric influence.
