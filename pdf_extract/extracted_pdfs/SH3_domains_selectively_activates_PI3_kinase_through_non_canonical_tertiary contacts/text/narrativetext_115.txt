Our results suggested that the interaction of Fyn SH3 with p85 involved regions on both proteins outside of the canonical SH3-PR contacts. For Fyn SH3, these regions were the PR-proximal position 96 and the PR-distal position 112, whereas for p85 it was its nSH2 and cSH2 domains. Given that p85 nSH2 and cSH2 regulate the catalytic activity of the p110 subunit, we performed lipid kinase assays to assess the impact of SH3 binding. These assays were performed based on endogenous p110a protein immunopre- cipitated from endometrial cancer cells (EFE-184) which carry WT p110a and p85a. Immunoprecipitation (IP) followed by mass spectrometry data and western blot analysis demonstrated that our protocol only pulled down p110a but not the other p110 isoforms [Supplementary Fig. 7A-B; Sup- plementary Data 1; see ‘Methods’]. As a positive control, we used the PDGER pY phosphopeptide. The pY peptide at concentrations of 2 uM and 10 uM activated PI3K in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 5A), consistent with the Kz of approximately 660 nM for the interaction between pY and p85ASH3 [see entries in light blue in Fig. 3C; Supplementary Fig. 3].
