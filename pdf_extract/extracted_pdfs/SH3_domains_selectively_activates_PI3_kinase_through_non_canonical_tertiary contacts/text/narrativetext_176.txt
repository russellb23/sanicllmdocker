Because an experimental all-domain structure of PI3K was not available, we generated a model of this complex using AlphaFold*’. The predicted p110:p85 com- plex closely matched experimental structures, reproducing the observed positions of p85 nSH2 and iSH2 (Supplementary Fig. 10A, B). In this AlphaFold model, the cSH2 domain is positioned next to the kinase domain, similar to the inhibitory cSH2 position identified in the crystal structure of the PI3B heterocomplex (PDB id 2Y3A)'°. However, unlike the PI3KB crystal structure, the cSH2 of the PI3Ka AlphaFold model was not in direct contact with the p110 kinase domain, consistent with the lack of density for this domain in experimental PI3Ka structures’’. Instead, cSH2 was flexibly anchored through a predicted interaction between the iSH2-cSH2 linker residues Y°’SLV and the p110a kinase domain, suggestive of a different inhibitory mechanism involving Y°’SLV (Supplementary Fig. 10C). The p85 BH and PRI regions were not predicted to stably associate with the rest of the complex, in line with previous reports’. This model showed that in the biological context of the membrane-anchored PI3K heterodimer most of the 17 p85 iSH2 lysines would not be available for interactions, except for those at the basis of the iSH2 coiled-coil structure (Fig. 7B). The absence of strong tertiary contributions of the iSH2 is consistent with the observation that both p85 chimeras closely reproduced the p85ASH3 interactions with Fyn SH3. This structural model is compatible with an activation SH3 domain engaging the nSH2, cSH2 and possibly the basis of the iSH2, in addition to the PR1. Considering that the Fyn SH3 positions 96 and 112, which were important for binding and activation, are located on different sides of the SH3 domain, it is conceivable that activation results from these positions contacting residues on the nSH2, iSH2 and cSH2 domains of p85. Thus, a plausible model based on our data is that upon binding to PRI, activating SH3 domains simultaneously engage canonical interactions with PRI and tertiary interactions with nSH2 and cSH2 (see model in Fig. 7C, D). By disrupting the association between p110 and the p85 SH2 domains through these tertiary interactions, the SH3 domain would activate PI3K. Thus, SH3-based activation would be conceptually similar to PI3K activa- tion through phospho-peptides (Supplementary 10D).
