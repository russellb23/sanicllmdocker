Evolution is driven in large part by gene duplication and diversification’. As a result, proteomes often contain hundreds of protein domains with similar structure and overlapping functions. Understanding how the interactions between these domains and their ligands can be selective is important’, in particular when a lack of selectivity can lead to severe pathologies. Here, we examine a case in which a ubiquitous and inherently non-selective domain, the Src homology (SH) 3 domain, regulates a kinase pathway implicated in disease.
