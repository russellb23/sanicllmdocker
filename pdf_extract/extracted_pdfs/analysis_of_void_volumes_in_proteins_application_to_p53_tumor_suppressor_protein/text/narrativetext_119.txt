We have investigated the possibility that p53 is unstable owing to its core domain being poorly packed. However, we have shown that this is not the case: p53 is no more loosely packed than many other proteins, most of which are much more stable. However, the decrease in packing quality observed in cavity-creating mutations may be sufficient to destabilize the marginally stable p53 and reduce its Tm below body temperature. It is worth noting, that the p53 core domain is relatively large at approxi- mately 200 residues in size and as such has a high enthalphy of denaturation. At 37 °C, its stability is only 3 kcal/mol, at 43 °C (the protein’s T,,), it is, by definition, 0 kcal/mol. Any alteration in the pack- ing of the core domain may well decrease stability enough (i.e. by 3 kcal/mol) to lower its T,, to 37 °C and cause 50% denaturation at body temperature.'°
