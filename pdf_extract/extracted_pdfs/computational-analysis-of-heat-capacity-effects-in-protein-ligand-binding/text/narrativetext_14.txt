ABSTRACT: Heat capacity effects in protein—ligand binding as measured by calorimetric experiments have recently attracted considerable attention, particularly in the field of enzyme inhibitor design. A significant negative heat capacity change upon ligand binding implies a marked temperature dependence of the binding enthalpy, which is of high relevance for attempts to optimize protein—ligand interactions. In this work, we address the question of how well such heat capacity changes can be predicted by computer simulations. We examine a series of human thrombin inhibitors that all bind with AC, values of about —0.4 kcal/mol/ K and calculate heat capacity changes from plain molecular dynamics simulations of the bound and free states of the enzyme and ligand. The results show that accurate AC, estimates within a few tenths of a kcal/mol/K of the experimental values can be obtained with this approach. This allows us to address the structural and energetic origin of the negative heat capacity changes for the thrombin inhibitors, and it is found that conformational equilibria major contribution to the observed effect.
