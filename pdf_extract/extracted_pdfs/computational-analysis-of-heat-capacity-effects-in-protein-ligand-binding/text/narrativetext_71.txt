It can be noted that the above-mentioned loop region involving residues 141-155 (177-190 in Figure S) is in all cases the most mobile part of the structure and it thus makes sense that this region is not well resolved in the electron density maps. There is a small increase in the mobility of the loop region around residue 86 (Trp60D in the Bode et al. numbering)~” for the apo structures (both MD and X-ray), which presumably reflects the fact that this tryptophan side chain makes contact with the inhibitors. The average MD structures, however, do not show any significant differences in this region. Likewise, the MD structures (both apo and holo) show a slightly increased mobility around residue 229 (Asp186A), which appears affected by crystal contacts in the experimental structures. In general, however, the agreement between calculated and experimental RMSFs is very good. We can thus conclude that differences in protein flexibility between the different complexes and the unliganded enzyme do not either seem to explain the origin of the negative binding heat capacities. In fact, Winquist et al.° noted that since the AC values were statistically indistinguishable between the different ligand complexes, the negative value(s) is not likely to derive from the P3 substituent of the ligands, which is the only part that differs. Our analysis, so far, indicates that the negative AC, does not either derive from the $1 and S2 pockets of the enzyme since the structures and RMSFs compared to the apoenzyme do not really show any differences in the simulations.
