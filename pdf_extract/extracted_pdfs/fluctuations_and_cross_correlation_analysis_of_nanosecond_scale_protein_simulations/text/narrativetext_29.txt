The secondary structure is very stable throughout the simulation, as was checked using the DSSP program (Kabsch & Sander, 1983) for configurations taken every 10 ps. Residues 48 to 51 always form an a-helix, which extends up to residue 54 in more than 90% of the configurations and up to residue 55 in 13% of the configurations. Residues 19 to 23 and 30 to 34 always adopt an antiparallel B-sheet structure, to which the pairs 24-29 and 18-35 are associated in 94% of the configurations. A hydrogen-bonded bridge is ob- served in all configurations between Tyr21 and Phe45. The N-terminal 3!° helix (residues 3 to 6) makes an exception to this overall high stability. In about 70% of the cases, DSSP describes it as a 3'° helix from residues 3 to 5 (extended to Leu6 in only 43% of the cases), in 13% of the cases as an o-helix, and the rest of the time as a hydrogen-bonded turn.
