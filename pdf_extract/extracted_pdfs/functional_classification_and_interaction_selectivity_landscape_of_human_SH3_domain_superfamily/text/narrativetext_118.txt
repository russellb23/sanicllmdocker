SH3 domains are small protein interaction modules that are involved in numerous fundamental cellular processes and associated with the development of several diseases, in- cluding Joubert syndrome, leukemia, lymphoma, Usher syndrome or non-syndromic deaf- ness, centronuclear myopathy, schizophrenia, and other neurodevelopmental disorders [4]. Over the past three decades, researchers have focused on how members of the SH3DCP superfamily selectively recognize and bind to their associated PRM-containing proteins.
