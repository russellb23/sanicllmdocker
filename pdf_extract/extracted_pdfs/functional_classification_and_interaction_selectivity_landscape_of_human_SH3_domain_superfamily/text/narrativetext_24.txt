In the context of SH3 domain interaction with PRMs, a specific contact recognition occurs, where a positively charged PRM residue binds to conserved negatively charged residues in the variable loop region of the SH3 domain, resulting in moderate selectivity and affinity [5,16]. Comparative analysis of SH3 domain binding sites reveals remarkable variability and flexibility in the loop regions, contributing to the specificity and affinity of PRM binding [6,19]. The preference of the SH3 domain for specific PRMs is usually moderate, with affinities typically in the low micromolar range [20-22]. Furthermore, SH3 domains exhibit a broad spectrum of both conventional (PRM-based) and nonconventional selectivity, effectively recognizing a diverse array of protein interactors in a differentiated manner [4,5].
