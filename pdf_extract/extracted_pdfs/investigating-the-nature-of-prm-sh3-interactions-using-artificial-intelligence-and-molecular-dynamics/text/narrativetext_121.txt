It is generally observed that upon each proline-to-alanine mutation, the N-terminus and C-terminus make more frequent intrapeptide contacts as the structural constraint because the imine nature of proline has been relaxed. If this intrapeptide interaction is significant, then it will reduce the binding affinity between the peptide and SH3. We found that the position of the mutation determines the efficiency of the intrapeptide interaction. The C-terminus (residues 13-15) makes more global contacts with the other part of the peptide in the POA system than in the P7A system. As the “stapling” position determines the ratio between the N-terminal and C-terminal halves, we suggest that the split that P9A makes is more effective in inducing transient interactions between the N-terminus and C-terminus, which indicates a reduction of the competing interaction, i.e., the protein—peptide interaction.
