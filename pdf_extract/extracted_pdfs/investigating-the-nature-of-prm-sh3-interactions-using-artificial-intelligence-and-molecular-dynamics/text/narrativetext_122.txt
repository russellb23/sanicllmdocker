Collectively, in the PRM(H) series, the proline-to-alanine mutations play two roles: (i) it reduces the interaction between the mutated residue and the protein surface (direct effect), and (ii) the increased flexibility enhances transient intrapeptide interactions, which compete with the peptide—protein inter- action (indirect effect). The mutational position determines the mode and extent of intrapeptide interactions, hence leading to the position dependence of the mutational effect. In the case of the double mutant P7AP9A, which experimentally showed similar binding affinity with P9A, although the direct effect is doubled, the intrapeptide interactions are not as efficient as POA (Figure Si) and presumably compensate for the increase in the direct effect.
