Proteins interact with various biomolecules, including other proteins and peptides in living cells. Among them, protein— peptide interactions play a crucial role in many physiological processes, such as signal transduction,’ drug delivery,” and immune response.” Unlike canonical protein—protein inter- actions, which typically involve well-folded protein domains, protein—peptide interactions involve short peptides, usually consisting of S—20 amino acids. Upon binding, most peptides leverage their flexibility to maximize the binding interface, often forming hydrogen bonds with backbone atoms.*
