Subsequent NPT simulations were also performed at 300 K for an additional 100 ps to further equilibrate the system, employing both the Berendsen barostat”® and the velocity rescaling thermostat.”” During both NVT and NPT equilibration processes, positional restraints were applied to the heavy atoms of the protein and peptide with spring constants of 1000 kJ/mol-nm’, allowing the solvent to equilibrate around the protein. After NPT equilibration, production MD simulations were carried out without restraints for 100 ns. The first 10 ns were considered as the equilibration period and were discarded from the analysis. To ensure robust results, all simulations were repeated 10 times under the same computational conditions for all complexes (total 0.9 ys per system). All simulations were initiated from the same AlphaFold2-predicted structure. Energy minimization was carried out using the steepest descent method, ensuring that the structures across all 10 simulations were identical at this stage. The velocity distributions were generated using the Maxwell—Boltzmann distribution at 300 K, with distinct random seeds. Following this, each NVT equilibrium simulation was started using the generated velocity distribution. The simulations of the unbound PRM were performed using only the PRM structure, while those of the bound PRM structures were carried out using the PRM:SH3 complex. The molecular mechanics/Poisson—Boltzmann surface area (MM/PBSA) method” was used to calculate the solvation free energy (G,.1) of the peptides in their bound states.
