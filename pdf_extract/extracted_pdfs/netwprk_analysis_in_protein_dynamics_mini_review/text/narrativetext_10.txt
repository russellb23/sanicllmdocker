Random networks have a Poissonian degree distribu- tion, which means that they have a negligible amount of hubs. On the contrary, in many networks we observe a scale- free degree distribution, which means that the probability to find a hub with a number of neighbors a magnitude higher is a magnitude lower (but, importantly, not negligible). Net- works can be dissected to overlapping modules (communities, groups), which often form a hierarchical structure [1-7].
