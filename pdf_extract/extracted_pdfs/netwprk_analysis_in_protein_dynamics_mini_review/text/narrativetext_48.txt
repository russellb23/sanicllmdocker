As an example for the use of protein structural networks for the analysis of protein dynamics, fluctuations of amino acid side-chains are correlated with the mean of the shortest path lengths of the amino acid in the protein structural network [17]. This reflects that more central amino acids (having a shorter average of their shortest path lengths) have a more re- stricted motion. Protein structural networks take into account only the interactions between amino acid side-chains, and ne- glect the constraints of the protein backbone. This is not a problem, if we analyze the topology of these networks, and want to draw conclusions for the structure and stability of pro- teins. However, it may restrict the analysis, when we would like to use the dynamics of topological networks to explain protein motions and rearrangements. This problem is circumvented by the elastic network model, where only the atomic coordinates of the «C atoms are used to build the network.
