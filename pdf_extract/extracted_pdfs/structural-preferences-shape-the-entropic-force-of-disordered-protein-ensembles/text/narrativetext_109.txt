To do this, we normalize the average R,. of all IDRs against the R,. of a GS repeat sequence of the same length to get normalized end-to-end distance R,, (eq 1, Materials and Methods section). R,, has a negative value when the ensemble is more compact than a GS repeat and a positive value when an ensemble is more expanded. We plot AS/kg for each sequence as a function of this normalized distance in Figure SB. It_is immediately noticeable that the vertical red line drawn at R,, = 0 separates sequences with a higher entropic force (purple markers) from those with a weaker entropic force (green markers). This means ensembles that are on average more compact than an equivalent GS repeat (as indicated by a negative R,.) tend to generate a stronger entropic force, while more expanded ensembles tend to generate a weaker entropic force than equivalent GS repeats.
